2. Markdown

In your document, describe your PhD project with a ### heading, one bold and one italic word, a 3-item list and a link to your lab.

3. Vectors

Stroop task, one participant, RT in ms:

rt <- c(523, 487, 601, 455, 2310, 512, 498, 577, 466, 540)
condition <- c("cong", "incong", "incong", "cong", "incong",
               "cong", "incong", "incong", "cong", "cong")
  1. Number of trials; mean, median, min, max RT.
  2. Find the suspicious trial (which.max()). Why do mean and median differ?
  3. Create rt_clean without trials over 2000 ms. Recompute the mean.
  4. Mean RT for "cong" and "incong" trials, without the slow one. Hint: drop it from both vectors.
  5. Convert rt to seconds (rt_s).

4. Missing values and comparisons

A second participant missed two trials:

rt2 <- c(523, NA, 487, 150, 601, 455, 2310, NA, 512, 498)
  1. How many NAs? In which trials?
  2. Mean RT, ignoring them.
  3. Flag implausible trials: faster than 200 ms or slower than 1500 ms. How many? What proportion of the non-missing trials? Hint: the mean of a logical vector is a proportion.
  4. Print rt2[rt2 > 200]. What went wrong? Keep only the trials that are both non-missing and plausible.

6. Factors: a Likert item

“How often do you have trouble sleeping?”

answers <- c("often", "never", "sometimes", "always", "often", "rarely",
             "sometimes", "often", "never", "always", "sometimes", "often")
  1. table(answers). Is the order sensible?
  2. Make answers_f, an ordered factor with levels from "never" to "always". table() again.
  3. Scores 1 to 5: as.numeric(answers_f). Mean score?
  4. A new participant answers "Often". Add it, rebuild the factor. What happens, and why?

7. A data frame by hand

id group age mmse
P01 patient 72 21
P02 control 65 29
P03 patient 68 23
P04 control 70 28
P05 patient 61 25
P06 control 74 27
  1. Build it as participants (group as a factor). Check with str().
  2. Add a column impaired: TRUE if mmse is below 24.
  3. Patients older than 65.
  4. Mean mmse per group (tapply()).
  5. Sort the rows by age (order()).

8. Your first function

  1. Write se(), the standard error of the mean, \(s/\sqrt{n}\). Use it on rt_clean.
  2. Make it ignore NAs, and try it on rt2. Hint: sd(x, na.rm = TRUE), sum(!is.na(x)).
  3. Write n_outliers(x, low = 200, high = 1500): the number of values below low or above high, ignoring NAs. Try n_outliers(rt2) and n_outliers(rt2, high = 600).

9. A data frame: sleep

Built into R: extra hours of sleep (extra) for 10 patients (ID) under two drugs (group).

  1. str(), head(), summary(). Rows and columns?
  2. Mean extra per drug (tapply(), or subset twice).
  3. Every patient took both drugs. Per patient, compute drug 2 - drug 1. How many are positive?
  4. Write this sentence in your document, with inline code in place of the dots:
On average, drug 2 increased sleep by `{r} ...` hours more than drug 1,
and `{r} ...` out of 10 patients slept more with drug 2.

10. A figure with a caption

  1. boxplot(extra ~ group, data = sleep).
  2. Add #| label: fig-... and #| fig-cap:. Reference it in the text with @fig-.... Render.
  3. Add #| echo: false. What changes?

11. Read data from the project

sleepstudy (Belenky et al., 2003): mean reaction time per day for 18 subjects. Days 0 and 1 were training, day 2 is the baseline. After day 2, subjects spent only 3 hours in bed per night.

  1. read.csv(here::here("data", "sleepstudy.csv")).
  2. How many subjects? (unique(), length())
  3. Mean reaction time at baseline (day 2) and on day 9?
  4. Per subject, the change from day 2 to day 9. Hint: keep the rows of day 2 and of day 9 in two data frames; check that Subject is in the same order; subtract.
  5. How many subjects slowed down? Who slowed down the most?
  6. hist() of the changes, with a figure caption.
  7. One sentence with the mean change and the number of subjects who slowed down, all with inline code.
