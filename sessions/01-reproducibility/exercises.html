Session 1: Exercises

R and Quarto basics

Author

Giuseppe

Published

November 22, 2026

Part 1 is plain R, in a script. In Part 2 you turn your analysis into a Quarto report.

Data (save into data/ in your project): sleepstudy.csv

No R on your laptop yet?

Use R in the browser, no login needed: https://webr.sh.

  • Part 1 works there. In exercise 8, read the data from the web: read.csv("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/giuseppealfonzetti/s4ns-A/main/data/sleepstudy.csv")
  • Part 2 needs RStudio: pair with a neighbour.

Install R and RStudio (setup) before session 2.

Part 1: R

Open your course project, then File > New File > R Script and save it as session1.R. Run a line with Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter on a Mac).

1. Reaction times

One participant, ten trials of a Stroop task, reaction times in ms:

rt <- c(523, 487, 601, 455, 2310, 512, 498, 577, 466, 540)
condition <- c("cong", "incong", "incong", "cong", "incong",
               "cong", "incong", "incong", "cong", "cong")
  1. How many trials? What are the mean, median, min and max?
  2. One trial looks odd. Which one? Use which.max(). Why are the mean and the median so different?
  3. Make rt_clean, without trials over 2000 ms. What is the mean now?
  4. What is the mean for "cong" and for "incong" trials, without the odd one? Drop it from both vectors, so they stay aligned.
  5. Convert rt to seconds.

2. Missing trials

A second participant missed two trials:

rt2 <- c(523, NA, 487, 150, 601, 455, 2310, NA, 512, 498)
  1. How many trials are missing, and which ones?
  2. What is the mean without them?
  3. Trials faster than 200 ms or slower than 1500 ms are implausible. How many are there? What proportion of the non-missing trials? The mean of TRUE/FALSE values is a proportion.
  4. Try rt2[rt2 > 200]. Why do NAs appear? Keep only the trials that are present and plausible.

3. Spot the bugs

This code has five mistakes. Fix them. One gives no error, just a wrong answer: which one?

scores <- c(12, 15, 9, 20, 17)
group <- c("a", "b", "a", "b", "a")
mean(scores[group = "a"]
maen(scores[group == "b"])
Mean(scores)
summary(Scores)

4. A Likert item

Twelve answers to “How often do you have trouble sleeping?”:

answers <- c("often", "never", "sometimes", "always", "often", "rarely",
             "sometimes", "often", "never", "always", "sometimes", "often")
  1. Run table(answers). Does the order make sense?
  2. Make answers_f, an ordered factor from "never" to "always". Run table() again.
  3. as.numeric(answers_f) turns the answers into scores from 1 to 5. What is the mean score?
  4. Someone answers "Often". Add it and rebuild the factor. What happens?

5. A data frame by hand

id group age mmse
P01 patient 72 21
P02 control 65 29
P03 patient 68 23
P04 control 70 28
P05 patient 61 25
P06 control 74 27
  1. Type it in as participants, with group as a factor. Check it with str().
  2. Add a column impaired: TRUE when mmse is below 24.
  3. Which patients are older than 65?
  4. What is the mean mmse in each group? Try tapply().
  5. Sort the rows by age with order().

6. Your first functions

  1. Write se(), the standard error of the mean, \(s/\sqrt{n}\). Try it on rt_clean.
  2. Now try it on rt2. Make it ignore NAs: sd(x, na.rm = TRUE) and sum(!is.na(x)) help.
  3. Write n_outliers(x, low = 200, high = 1500), which counts the values below low or above high. Try n_outliers(rt2), then n_outliers(rt2, high = 600).

7. A drug trial: sleep

sleep comes with R: extra hours of sleep (extra) for 10 patients (ID) under two drugs (group).

  1. Look at it with str(), head() and summary(). How many rows and columns?
  2. What is the mean extra for each drug?
  3. Every patient took both drugs. For each patient, compute drug 2 minus drug 1. How many slept more with drug 2?

8. Sleep deprivation: sleepstudy

Reaction times of 18 subjects, one mean per day (Belenky et al., 2003). Days 0 and 1 were training and day 2 is the baseline. After day 2, subjects spent only 3 hours in bed per night.

  1. Read it: sleepstudy <- read.csv(here::here("data", "sleepstudy.csv")).
  2. How many subjects are there?
  3. What is the mean reaction time on day 2, and on day 9?
  4. For each subject, compute the change from day 2 to day 9. Put the two days in two data frames, check that Subject is in the same order, then subtract.
  5. How many subjects slowed down? Who slowed down the most?
  6. Draw a hist() of the changes.

Part 2: Quarto

9. Your first report

  1. File > New File > Quarto Document. Put your name as author, save it as session1-report.qmd and click Render.
  2. In the header, replace format: html with the lines below and render again. What changed?
format:
  html:
    toc: true
    toc-location: left
  1. Write a few lines: a ### heading, one bold and one italic word, a short list and a link to a website.

10. Bring in your analysis

  1. Copy your exercise 8 code into chunks in the report. Render.
  2. Give the histogram chunk a label and a caption (#| label: fig-change, #| fig-cap: "..."). Refer to it in the text with @fig-change.
  3. Add #| echo: false to the chunk that reads the data. What changes?

11. Numbers in the text

  1. Write one sentence with the mean change and the number of subjects who slowed down. Use inline code, as in `{r} round(mean(change))`, never typed numbers.
  2. Copy your sleep code from exercise 7 into the report and complete this sentence:
On average, patients slept `{r} ...` hours more with drug 2 than with drug 1,
and `{r} ...` out of 10 slept more with drug 2.

12. Does it render from scratch?

  1. In the console, type x <- 42. In the report, add a chunk with x * 2. Render. What happens, and why?
  2. Delete that chunk, then Session > Restart R and render again. Does everything still work?
  3. Add a last chunk with sessionInfo().

Bonus: PDF and Word

  1. A PDF needs LaTeX. Install TinyTeX once, in the RStudio Terminal (not the Console): quarto install tinytex.
  2. Add two formats to the header:
format:
  html:
    toc: true
    toc-location: left
  pdf: default
  docx: default
  1. Render all three in the Terminal: quarto render session1-report.qmd. Open the PDF and the Word file. What differs from the HTML?
